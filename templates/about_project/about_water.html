Water Quality Variables Explained

Salinity (ppt) – Parts per Thousand

What it is: The concentration of dissolved salts in water.

Why it matters: High salinity can affect the health of aquatic organisms. Freshwater ecosystems usually require very low salinity, while estuarine or marine environments tolerate more.

Indicator of: Pollution, tidal influence, or evaporation levels.

Dissolved Oxygen (mg/L)

What it is: Amount of oxygen dissolved in water, necessary for aquatic life.

Why it matters: Low DO can lead to "dead zones" where life can't survive. High DO usually indicates good water quality.

Indicator of: Biological health, organic pollution, or temperature effects.

pH (standard units)

What it is: A measure of how acidic or basic the water is (scale 0–14).

Why it matters: Most aquatic life thrives in a pH range of 6.5–8.5. Too high or low can harm organisms or indicate chemical contamination.

Indicator of: Acid rain, industrial discharge, or natural buffering.

Secchi Depth (m)

What it is: A measure of water clarity using a Secchi disk.

Why it matters: Lower values suggest turbidity or algal blooms, reducing light penetration and photosynthesis.

Indicator of: Sediment levels, plankton density, or pollution.

Water Depth (m)

What it is: The depth at the sampling site.

Why it matters: Affects temperature stratification, oxygen levels, and sampling consistency.

Indicator of: Context for interpreting other parameters like temperature and oxygen.

Water Temperature (°C)

What it is: Temperature of the water at the time of sampling.

Why it matters: Influences dissolved oxygen, metabolism of organisms, and chemical reactions.

Indicator of: Seasonal variation, thermal pollution, or mixing.

Time (24:00)

What it is: The time of sampling (in 24-hour format).

Why it matters: Diurnal cycles can affect DO, temperature, and photosynthesis.

Indicator of: Temporal variation in water properties.

Air Temperature (°C)

What it is: Ambient air temperature at the time of sampling.

Why it matters: Affects water temperature and evaporation.

Indicator of: Weather conditions or seasonal effects.

Year

What it is: The year when the sample was taken.

Why it matters: Tracks long-term trends and changes in water quality over time.

Indicator of: Climate change, policy impacts, or long-term pollution.
